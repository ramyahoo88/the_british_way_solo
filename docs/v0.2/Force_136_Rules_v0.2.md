# Force 136: a card-driven opponent for Japan

Version 0.2 | 20 November 2026 | Unofficial playable prototype

## A paper opponent for Japan

### Scope and components

You play Japan in the standard standalone Japanese Occupation scenario. Force 136 is controlled by this original fan-made prototype; the MPAJA remains the game's existing independent actor. You need The British Way, Enemy of My Enemy, their physical Event cards and player aids, and the 18 bot cards. No application is required. Campaign linking, Pre-War Options and Faction Personas are outside v0.2.

### Set up

Use the published scenario setup without bonuses for either side. Shuffle the bot cards separately. Leave room for one active bot card and a bot discard pile. Use the printed map and railway orders on the reference sheet. The labels A, B and D in the Event guide are bot preferences, not new game rules. The bot begins with no active card.

### The bot turn

First finish the physical card's MPAJA icon and any Mandatory Event. Resolve them in the printed order and end the game immediately if instructed. Japan makes the choices assigned to Japan; use page 6 for British choices. When the British normal turn begins, draw a bot card only if there is no active one. Then follow the steps below.

### 0. Check known final scoring

Only in campaign 3, after Japan has acted, with exactly one card remaining in the physical Event deck: use the bounded check on page 9 before the Event. Never inspect that card. Six Events in a campaign do not prove Propaganda is next.

### 1. Check the Event

If the Event box is legally available, consult its J-number. Take an applicable A Event. Take a B Event only if the active bot card says B EVENTS: YES. For D, use the denial rule on page 9. A skipped or unavailable Event sends you to step 2. AUTO Events never offer the Event box. Use the physical card's text for execution.

### 2. Check two urgent needs

If no Force 136 remains on the map, try a productive Contact before the printed task. In campaign 3, if Resistance is at least 16 and Camps are at least as numerous as on-map Force 136, try SUPPLY first, or a Contact that places an Available Force 136 if Supply is unavailable. These are decision priorities only: they grant no extra action. If neither need can be addressed legally, continue.

### 3. Attempt the card

Try the primary task, then preparation tasks from left to right, using pages 3-5. Skip an option with no permitted useful result. A primary task needing a Special Activity requires an Operation plan that preserves a useful target for it. Select the Initiative box using page 2: one Operation and at most one Special Activity. Recheck targets after every change.

### 4. Keep or discard

Discard for a primary attempted through the normal task route, even on a failed roll or zero Intel. An Event never completes the primary, even if its free actions match it. Other first turns rotate and retain the card. After that second turn discard it regardless. Free actions and MPAJA choices never age the card. Draw a replacement only at the next normal British turn. Shuffle the discard pile only when a draw is needed and the bot draw pile is empty.

## How to resolve every choice

### Useful means a real opportunity

A recruit or refresh, a new useful Force 136 position, a legal new Sabotage marker, a permitted Ambush with a useful target, a cache placement, a new Intel reveal, or a Raid that can attack or gain a majority is useful. Unknown Intel counts as an opportunity regardless of its eventual value. Never look beneath a concealed chit, inspect future Events, invent a success or reroll a failure. A pure Contact hold may accompany a useful Special Activity.

### Initiative and the single action budget

SUPPLY, INTEL and RAID as primary tasks require the Operation & Special Activity box. If it is unavailable, try preparation. For RAIL, STRIKE, TRAIN or a preparation Operation, choose Operation & Special Activity only if a compatible Special Activity will remain useful after the planned Operation, in the card's printed order; otherwise choose Limited Operation if available; otherwise choose Operation & Special Activity without a Special Activity if available. If no productive action can be taken, Pass in the leftmost legal box and use Contact if useful. Keep the mandatory-card Event restriction. Follow the printed cylinder order for next eligibility.

### Special Activity timing

Resolve the Operation first and then the one chosen Special Activity. Forecast deterministic placements, moves and activations before choosing the box; do not roll or reveal Intel while planning. The bot forgoes interleaving. For SUPPLY use Contact then Supply; for INTEL use Contact then Gather; for RAID use Train if useful, otherwise Contact, then Raid. A Contact movement option may move no markers when the existing positions support the Special Activity. After an ordinary Operation, choose the first useful compatible Special Activity from the card; if none is compatible and useful, omit it. Never add another Special Activity after a primary SUPPLY, INTEL or RAID.

### Map order and ties

Apply only the priorities stated for the decision, in order. Break remaining space ties by: Kedah, Perak, Kelantan, Trengganu, Pahang, Selangor, Kuala Lumpur, Negri Sembilan, Johore, northern Economic Center, southern Economic Center. Economic Centers have no Population or Intel. Treat their Population as zero for a preference only. Thailand, Singapore and Indonesia are not playable spaces. A specified reverse map order reverses this list. With no active card, use rail order C and Contact toward Supply if an Available cache exists, otherwise Intel.

### Opposition priorities

Among legal neutral targets, prefer a space whose Opposition will count normally at Propaganda, then larger Population, then no Terror, then map order. The Japanese side of J15 can make a New Village's Opposition ineffective at Propaganda without removing its marker. Terror is a prohibition only when the particular action says so; do not impose it on every Event effect. For removal of the bot's Opposition when a choice is assigned to Britain, reverse this preference.

### Protect donors and avoid clashes

For voluntary guerrilla relocations, preserve the last guerrilla at a cache and do not break a current strict guerrilla majority at the source. Do not mix red and blue through voluntary placement or movement. Exceptions are mandatory effects and effects with no legal alternative. These are bot choices, not new restrictions on Japan. One piece can be voluntarily placed or moved at most once during a given Operation or Raid. Do not recycle a cache from the map voluntarily.

### Forced losses and global choices

When Britain chooses its own forced guerrilla loss: active before underground; then a minority color in a mixed group; then a space without a cache; then most guerrillas; then reverse map order. A tie between colors at one space loses blue first. Lose a cache only when the effect requires it or no eligible guerrilla can be lost. If forced to return a cache to Zipper after Available caches are exhausted, choose the cache with fewest guerrillas, then most Troops, then reverse map order.

## Contact and Train

### CONTACT: choose a plan

For INTEL, hold when current anchors already permit a useful Gather; this overrides placement or movement below. Otherwise prefer placing an Available Force 136 in a Province without Force 136. If none is Available, move one on-map Force 136 to an adjacent playable space without Force 136, but only to escape exposure, enable the current primary task, or open a future Supply site. Do not relocate wooden red guerrillas with Contact. Do not exchange a marker from another place for an Available marker. A zero-move Contact is allowed only to accompany a useful Special Activity or as an unproductive Pass benefit.

### Contact destination priorities

First prefer no Kempeitai. Next prefer a site useful for this card's primary task: SUPPLY = a Province without a cache when an Available cache exists; INTEL = most concealed chits that could actually be Gathered from this new position; RAIL = an existing underground guerrilla beside a clear railway; STRIKE = an existing underground guerrilla with a useful attack; TRAIN = a cache, then a Camp; RAID = a cache with guerrillas to send. Next prefer more guerrillas, then larger Population, then map order. A placement cannot carry a blue guerrilla, so do not count an imagined escort when assessing it.

### When moving a marker

Among markers able to make a useful move, choose an exposed one first, then one unable to contribute to its primary task, then one without a cache, then one with fewer guerrillas, then reverse map order. A marker is exposed when Kempeitai share its space and could target it: no Camp protects it, or the Japanese J22 capability is active. Re-evaluate a destination using only the pieces that can legally be there.

### Blue escort and Camps

Take one blue guerrilla with a moving marker if the destination has no red guerrilla and the donor protection rule permits it. Choose underground before active. After an actual Contact placement or move, resolve the printed Camp instruction. If a Camp must be recycled, choose a Camp without Force 136, then one with a cache, then smaller Population, then reverse map order. Do not generate a Camp when no marker moved. Event and Propaganda marker moves create no Camps unless specifically instructed.

### TRAIN: recruit or refresh

Select every Force 136 space where Train can make a useful legal placement, even during a British Limited Train. Match its existing color. At a mixed site reinforce the larger group; a tie favors red. If that color is illegal, skip the site. At an empty site prefer legal red, otherwise blue. Do not create a mixed group merely to use spare pieces. At a cache use the printed larger allowance, applying the color choice to each placement.

### If the preferred color is unavailable

First refresh an active guerrilla of that color in the same space if useful. Otherwise take a same-color donor with no Force 136, respecting donor protection: active donors first, then more Japanese cubes, then reverse map order. Preserve pieces needed for this turn's primary Raid. If neither exists, another color is permissible only in an empty destination and only if legal. Otherwise skip. Never reuse a guerrilla twice in one Train.

### Placement priorities for Events and automatic recruits

When the effect lets Britain choose placement spaces, prefer: a Force 136 space with no guerrilla; a cache with no guerrilla; a destination where one recruit creates a strict majority; then a Force 136 space; then a cache; then fewer Japanese cubes; then larger Population; then map order. Apply compatible colors first. If pieces run short, process eligible spaces in this order. A fixed or mandatory destination overrides color preferences; do not silently cancel a forced mixed group.

## Supply, Gather and railway attacks

### SUPPLY: a Contact with a purpose

The primary SUPPLY task uses Contact and Supply. It is useful if a legal Contact plan or a zero-move hold leaves a Camp that can be converted with an Available cache. Choose a Contact destination for Supply, then resolve Supply in all useful permitted spaces. Prefer no Kempeitai, then more guerrillas, then larger Population, then map order. Use the applicable printed space allowance and capabilities. Never place a second cache in a space or place one in an Economic Center. A failed J8 roll consumes that attempt.

### INTEL: reach the evidence

The primary INTEL task uses Contact and Gather. Contact holds if current positions already give a useful Gather, before considering an Available marker or a move. Otherwise place or move using the Intel destination priorities. For Gather, choose a Force 136 anchor that can reveal the most distinct eligible concealed chits, then one with its own eligible concealed chit, then no Kempeitai, then map order. Resolve it before choosing the next anchor so overlaps are not counted twice. Use every legal reveal from that anchor, taking target spaces in map order. Each chit is revealed at most once.

### Gather restrictions matter

Count only spaces with the required underground guerrilla; do not count all neighbors as automatic Intel. Activate red underground before blue unless the effect specifies a color; with J22 apply its printed exception. With Japanese J19, the first selected anchor is the one allowed to use adjacent guerrillas; later anchors use only their own space. With British J19 use its larger anchor allowance. Known zero-value revealed chits are not concealed and give no new Gather opportunity. Concealed values never enter target selection.

### RAIL: use Sabotage

Choose every useful eligible Force 136 space, in map order, and assign an adjacent clear railway using the card's N, C or S rail order. Resolve one before selecting the next so two spaces do not attack the same newly damaged railway. Use the global activation priority on page 9: red underground before blue. With British J27, add one permitted non-Force-136 location using rail order, then map order. Ordinary Sabotage never adds a second marker to an already damaged railway.

### Live railway and regional order

Prefer a live railway: a clear one on an all-clear route between an Economic Center and a clear starred exit. Trace the public map; if none of the candidates is live, use regional order among other clear candidates. Orders are on the reference sheet. This preference changes neither connectivity nor adjacency. An Event permitting stacked damage targets fresh railways first, then fewest markers, then regional order. Select distinct railways within an Event unless its printed text explicitly allows repeats.

### After an ordinary Operation

Follow the card's Special Activity order only if Operation & Special Activity was selected and the pairing is legal. Train can add Supply or Raid; Contact can add Supply, Gather or Raid; Sabotage and Ambush can add Gather or Raid. Recheck after the Operation: its activated guerrillas may no longer Gather. If the planned Special Activity has become useless, another compatible one in the same card order may be chosen. Never redraw the bot card for a better result.

## Ambush and Raid

### STRIKE: identify useful attacks

Use each eligible Force 136 space with an underground guerrilla, activating red before blue (page 9). At a space, first try to displace Kempeitai when the shot is good; otherwise remove a Police if present; otherwise try to displace a Troop when the shot is good. A good shot succeeds on at least 3 of the 6 die results, counting the printed cache modifiers. Do not try to displace something that is already in Kuala Lumpur, and do not roll when success is impossible. Event J26 has its own outcome and is handled separately.

### Priorities between strike spaces

Prefer a useful Kempeitai displacement, then removing the last Police in a space, then another Police removal, then a useful Troop displacement. Within a category prefer the higher success chance, then more supporting caches, then map order. Removing Police is certain when legal. With British J27, add its permitted location without Force 136 using these same priorities. An attempted failed Ambush still completes the card's STRIKE task.

### RAID: one Special Activity

For the primary RAID task, use a useful Train first if possible; otherwise use a productive Contact or Contact hold. Then Raid. The ordinary card Special Activity option uses the same procedure. Each chosen destination needs an adjacent cache source that can supply one or two guerrillas without violating donor protection. Move each piece at most once. An incoming guerrilla can act under the Raid permission without Force 136; this permission is confined to that Raid destination.

### Choose a Raid destination

Prefer a destination where an incoming underground guerrilla can Sabotage a live clear railway; then one with another clear railway to Sabotage; then one with a useful STRIKE; then one where the move creates a strict guerrilla majority. Break rail ties by the card's regional order, strike ties by strike priorities, and other ties by map order. Recheck after each Raid destination. Do not select the same destination twice.

### Choose source and number

Among eligible adjacent cache sources, prefer one that can send the required pieces while keeping its majority and last cache defender; then the source with more remaining guerrillas; then map order. Send the fewest pieces needed: one underground guerrilla for a permitted attack, otherwise the minimum up to two needed to create a strict majority. If an attack needs an underground piece and only active pieces can be sent, that attack is not a candidate. Prefer the color already at the destination; at an empty destination prefer red. If no non-mixing transfer works, skip the destination.

### Perform the Raid attack

After moving, perform the chosen Limited Sabotage or Ambush with an incoming underground guerrilla, if still legal. A territorial Raid may omit the attack. Use the printed maximum number of Raid destinations and the Zipper modifier. J27 does not make the bot attack an additional remote space from a Raid. If moving guerrillas removes a New Village under the normal rule, remove it immediately.

### If the whole turn stalls

Do not reroll or draw again. Use the next preparation task on the active card. If none can do anything, Pass in the leftmost permitted Initiative box and take its normal Contact benefit if useful. A bot turn can deliberately have no board effect. Age the active card normally; a two-turn card can never lock the deck indefinitely.

## Decisions between normal turns

### MPAJA icons

Resolve the icon before the Mandatory Event or either normal player turn. For a British Mobilize Population choice, use Opposition priorities among the printed legal targets. For a British Kill Traitors choice, prefer removing the last Police in a space, then Police sharing a space with Force 136, then most revealed Intel value, then map order. Japan chooses its own Seize Supplies and Food Shortage targets. Do not draw, rotate or discard a bot card for an icon.

### Mandatory effects and Japanese Events

Use the physical card for the effect, eligible pieces and who makes each choice. Japan keeps its assigned choices. For a British optional action, use the matching priorities and act if useful; otherwise decline. A free choice of Operation tries RAIL, STRIKE, TRAIN, CONTACT; a free Special Activity tries SUPPLY, GATHER, RAID, within the effect's permissions. No new bot card is drawn. For forced losses use page 2. A Mandatory Event still closes the Event box when its immediate effect does nothing.

### Standing British capability decisions

With British J12, use Opposition priorities for the offered placement after each affected repair. With British J15, use the compatible-color rule for the offered guerrilla at each New Village after Extract. Neither is a new normal turn. Apply Japanese capability restrictions even if they make the current card fail. A physical Event always governs the legality and outcome of its actions; the bot guide only supplies choices.

### Propaganda: keep the published sequence

Complete the MPAJA phase and Resistance phase from the physical aid, including clashes before recruitment and the net score change. If this is the final scoring, stop before later phases. Otherwise make the British choices below at their published points, with Japan taking its intervening actions normally. No bot card is drawn, used or aged. At the end of a nonfinal round discard any unfinished bot card; keep the remaining deck and discard pile in their existing order.

### British Propaganda choices

For each damaged railway, use Opposition priorities among its eligible adjacent targets, resolving them one at a time. During the free Supply, use Supply priorities and current capabilities. For optional Force 136 redeployment, move one marker at a time to a distinct Camp space without Force 136: no Kempeitai, then a new Supply site with an Available cache, then more guerrillas, then larger Population, then map order. Move only if escaping exposure, leaving a space with neither guerrillas nor cache, or opening a new Supply site from a space that already has a cache. Staying is allowed; redeployment does not create Camps.

### Scoring and the third actor

Use section 7 of the published rules to decide the winner. In particular, a camp-count tie at Resistance 18-19 is not a British win. Record an MPAJA victory separately: Japan and the Force 136 bot both lose. This prototype adds no early victory when Resistance temporarily reaches a high value. Follow J9 when it causes final scoring before the expected end of campaign 3.

### Check the physical aid on reset

Apply the printed reset, capabilities, marker limits and automatic removals. Sabotage is not cleared merely because a campaign ended. The bot's card discard instruction adds no game effect. All compulsory placements, removals and track consequences still occur in the published order.

## Capabilities and a worked start

### A short capability checklist

Keep executed physical Capability cards visible. Before a matching action, check J8 for Supply eligibility or losses; J12 for Opposition after repair or repair during Extract; J15 for recruits during Extract or New Village treatment at Propaganda; J19 for Gather anchors and adjacency; J22 for Gather activation or marker-target order; and J27 for extra ordinary attack locations or Opposition loss during Supply. Use the printed wording and limits, including mandatory negative effects.

### An illustrative opening: card 01

Assume the published starting board and a current Event skipped by the Event guide. Card 01 wants SUPPLY. The British begin first eligible, so Operation & Special Activity is free. Contact brings in the Available Force 136. Kedah and Kelantan both have a blue guerrilla, no Kempeitai, no Force 136 and Population 2; map order selects Kedah. This placement carries no escort and adds a Camp. Supply can then convert the Camps in Kedah and Negri Sembilan using the two Available caches. Both have one guerrilla and Population 2, so Kedah is processed first. Perak already has a cache and cannot take another. Discard card 01.

### What this opening demonstrates

The bot can expand and Supply during one legal Contact/Supply turn, using one Operation and one Special Activity. This is a rules walk-through, not a recommendation that a human would always choose that opening. Playtests should check whether its modestly guarded caches need stronger protection and whether blue recruits are exposed to later MPAJA clashes. The draft leaves those decisions visible for feedback.

### An interrupted plan

An INTEL card is active but Japan has taken Operation & Special Activity. The bot cannot Gather this turn. It tries its first preparation task, TRAIN, using Limited Operation. The British Limited operation may cover multiple Force 136 spaces. Rotate the card sideways. On the next British normal turn it tries INTEL again. If a Gather is legally attempted it discards the card even if all newly revealed chits are zero. If Japan blocks the required box again, the bot prepares or Passes, then discards the card because it has had two turns.

### Three important checks

RAIL and Gather cannot both spend the same already-activated guerrilla without a printed exception. A Contact placement or move can generate a Camp; an ordinary Event relocation or Propaganda redeployment cannot. A cache can support Raid without Force 136 at the destination, but cannot allow a guerrilla to act from a space it never legally entered.

### Prototype status

This is a playable design draft, not a measured competitive release. Development checks cover the deck, initiative decisions and selected rules-sensitive decisions. They do not model complete games or establish playing strength. Focused checks now include the audit boards, public map adjacency and railway routes. No simulated win rate, physical playtest or balance claim is made. The bot uses deliberately bounded choices, including at most one Contact marker move and no voluntary cache recycling. J17 is deliberately skipped as an Event choice.

## First tabletop test and provenance

### A focused first session

Play one standard game with Japan pursuing a coherent plan, such as reliable extraction supported by Police and selective repair. Record every normal British task, failed or stalled turn, ambiguous choice, and card kept for a second turn. Separately record MPAJA losses. After each Reprisal note cache guards and the next recovery plan. At the end record the winner, Resistance and marker counts. Do not describe one result as a win rate.

### Then vary the Japanese plan

Try a second game focused on eliminating Force 136, and a third focused on railway defense and removing caches. Watch whether one repeated Japanese action strands the bot, whether it repeatedly builds undefended caches, whether it can recover its marker network, and whether it spends late turns feeding the MPAJA. Preserve the rules and card distribution during this first comparison.

### Measure administration

Time at least five normal bot turns after learning the procedures. Record the longest lookup, any choice requiring more than three comparisons, and any rule you have to read twice. The design target is most ordinary turns around a minute once learned; this has not been measured. Event and Propaganda turns may take longer. Prefer fixing wording or priorities before adding new tracks or tokens.

### Before calling it competitive

Validate a complete rules engine against the official example and scoring before running full-game simulations. Include the real Event deck, all six capabilities, the independent MPAJA, legal Japanese policies, public-information limits, and the final-phase stop. Test multiple purposeful Japanese strategies and adversarial repetition. Separate engine correctness, strategy quality and tabletop usability. A large sample of an incomplete model cannot settle those questions.

## Final scoring and global safeguards

### When to use the final-scoring check

Use this before the Event only in campaign 3, after Japan has finished its normal turn, when exactly one card remains in the physical Event deck and the third Propaganda has not appeared. That publicly proves the next card is final Propaganda. Do not inspect it. An Event count alone is insufficient: Events below an earlier Propaganda carry into the next campaign. J9 ends play immediately under its own text and never gives this extra turn.

### Compare at most five plans

Use legal boxes only. In order, forecast: (1) the normal route after skipping the Event, including urgent needs, primary or first useful preparation and its Special Activity; (2) SUPPLY using ordinary Contact and Supply priorities; (3) the Event the guide would normally take, if any; (4) a productive CONTACT and the first useful compatible Special Activity in the active card order. For J4 only, also compare its other printed option as plan 5. Skip unavailable or duplicate plans; do not search alternative targets.

### Qualify a certain win

Use a paper forecast; do not move pieces, roll or reveal chits. Only qualify plans whose complete result is determined from public information. Exclude any plan with a die result, a new concealed Intel reveal or another unresolved effect. Chits left concealed contribute zero. Apply every certain side effect, then Propaganda clashes, recruitment and the net Resistance change with current capabilities. Use the actual available red pool and the normal recruit priorities. Cap the net score at 0-20 only after summing it.

### Select and execute

A plan qualifies only if final Resistance is 20, or 18-19 with fewer Camps than on-map Force 136 after all its effects. Choose the first qualifying plan in the order above. If none qualifies, use the usual turn procedure; this check does not claim to find every possible win. Execute only the selected plan. A plan completes the primary only when it uses that printed task through the normal task route; a matching SUPPLY override also counts. Events never complete it. Follow the normal two-turn limit.

### Denial is a limited option

Use a listed D threat only if Japan still has a normal turn this round and Event is free. With no useful U option, use the existing D instruction. If a useful U is rated B but the active card declines B, reconsider the listed D threat only when the entire normal task route would Pass. Then select Event and Pass with normal Contact. A productive normal route takes precedence over this extra denial check. Never deny after Japan has acted. Forecast without rolling or revealing.

### One global activation priority

For Sabotage, Ambush and Gather, activate an eligible red underground guerrilla before blue at the chosen space. A printed color requirement overrides this rule. A Raid attack must instead use an incoming eligible guerrilla; among eligible incoming pieces use red before blue. Forced losses still use the separate active-first rule on page 2. INTEL holds Contact when current anchors already support a useful Gather; it does not place an Available marker first.

## Event decisions

### J1: Sook Ching [U]

A with at least 2 Terror markers; B with 1. Skip if Resistance already 20.

No bot target. Count markers without imagining future Japanese reprisals.

### J2: Greater East Asia Co-Prosperity Sphere [U]

A if at least 2 clear railways are eligible; B if 1.

Use rail order. Fresh damage first; only then add another marker to an eligible damaged railway.

### J3: Overseas Chinese Association [U]

A if at least 2 placements help without creating a mixed-color space; B if 1.

Place red with red, blue with blue or in an empty space. Use placement priorities. Skip rather than cause avoidable clashes.

### J4: Chin Peng [U / S]

A if U actually raises Resistance by at least 2 after the cap; B if by 1; skip U at 20. S is considered only by the final-scoring check on page 9.

Use actual track gain, not the raw number of eligible spaces. At known final scoring compare the resulting score as well as Camp counts; a Camp tie is not a British win.

### J5: Hill Rats [U]

A if at least 2 active guerrillas at Force 136 or cache spaces become useful underground; B if 1.

No selection. Count only pieces that could enable Sabotage, Gather or a useful Ambush.

### J6: Jungle is Neutral [AUTO]

Mandatory: resolve after the MPAJA icon. No Event box this round.

For a British loss, remove an active guerrilla before an underground one; remove a mixed-color minority first within that group; preserve a cache while any guerrilla can be lost. Markers are not wooden forces.

### J7: South East Asia Command [U]

A if Gather can reveal at least 2 concealed chits; B if it can reveal 1, or Zipper can advance.

Use Gather priorities. Apply the track change and all its consequences before the free Gather.

### J8: Air Drops [U]

A in campaigns 1-2. B early in campaign 3 if a Camp without Force 136 could be Supplied.

Use the printed capability whenever applicable; it adds no extra Supply slot. See capability checklist.

### J9: Atomic Bombings [AUTO]

Mandatory: obey the physical card before either normal turn. No Event box.

If it ends the game, stop the bot procedure immediately and use the final scoring sequence. Do not finish the campaign first.

### J10: Rationing System [U]

A with Japanese Resources at least 8; B with 2-7.

No target. With 0-1, skip. Use the physical card for its rounding instruction.

### J11: KMT Guerrillas [U]

A if 2 northern spaces can make useful attacks; B if 1.

In each permitted space use RAIL before STRIKE, following normal target priorities. This Event supplies its own permissions; it is not an ordinary Force 136 operation.

### J12: Labor Service Corps [U]

A in campaigns 1-2. B early in campaign 3 with Sabotage on the board.

When the capability offers Opposition after Japanese repairs, use Opposition priorities. This choice does not use a bot turn.

### J13: Lai Teck Betrayal [U]

A if 2 useful permitted attacks exist; B if 1. D if there is no useful U attack and the printed Japanese option could threaten the last Force 136 marker.

Choose attacks one at a time: RAIL, then STRIKE. Use only the guerrillas and permissions named on the physical card. Recheck after the first attack.

### J14: Secret Army [U]

A if at least 2 useful, non-mixing placements are possible; B if 1. Otherwise D if a cache on the map could be lost to the Japanese option.

Place only where instructed. If D, occupy Event and Pass with Contact instead of executing either option. Do not manufacture recruits by moving protected donors.

### J15: Resettlement Schemes [U]

A in campaigns 1-2. B early in campaign 3 if a New Village exists.

When the Japanese Extract, use the compatible-color placement rule for each offered recruit. Follow the printed eligibility, even without Force 136.

### J16: Freddie Spencer Chapman [U]

A if it brings an Available Force 136 marker into useful play, or moves a threatened marker to safety; B for another productive relocation.

Available markers before on-map moves. Select distinct Camp spaces, safe sites before hot sites, then ones useful for Supply, then more guerrillas, then map order. Do not create Camps with this Event.

### J17: Burma Theater [SKIP]

The bot never selects this Event in v0.2.

If Japan selects it, resolve the physical card and normal track consequences. The bot does not model the trade-off between the two theaters.

### J18: Thai Administration [U]

A if at least 2 useful, non-mixing placements are possible; B if 1.

Use only the eligible northern Provinces. Existing blue groups before empty spaces; avoid red groups. Use placement priorities.

### J19: Radios [U]

A in campaigns 1-2. B early in campaign 3 if 3 Force 136 spaces could contribute to Gather.

Apply the printed capability. If the Japanese version is in play, choose the one anchor allowed to use adjacent guerrillas first.

### J20: Lim Bo Seng [U]

B only late in a campaign, with at least 2 Force 136 on the map and 2 concealed chits, when an ordinary Gather could not reveal 2 this turn. Otherwise D if the Japanese option can remove the last Force 136.

For U remove the least productive, most exposed Force 136; reveal chits outside Kempeitai spaces first, then map order. Do not inspect concealed values. D means Event + Pass/Contact.

### J21: Malay Collaboration [U]

A if at least 2 useful blue placements avoid red groups; B if 1.

Use placement priorities, keeping blue recruits together where practical. Do not move blue pieces out of protected cache or majority spaces merely to use this Event.

### J22: Jikeidan System [U]

A in campaigns 1-2. B early in campaign 3 if Gather could use an underground guerrilla.

The positive capability changes activation, not Intel eligibility. The Japanese version changes which marker Eliminate may target; account for that when judging exposure.

### J23: Allied Bombing [U]

A if at least 2 clear railways remain; B if 1. If all are damaged, B for extra damage.

Use rail order. Fill distinct clear targets before stacking; when only damaged targets remain, choose the fewest markers, then rail order.

### J24: Victory in Europe [U]

A if the resulting free Supply can make at least 2 caches; B if it can make 1 or Zipper can advance.

Apply track consequences first, then Supply priorities. An extra cache released from the track is not automatically placed on the map.

### J25: John Davis [U]

A if Contact plus its free Special Activity can complete SUPPLY, INTEL or RAID; B for another productive Contact.

Try SUPPLY, then INTEL, then RAID. Take only one Special Activity. This is an Event turn: age the card once without completing its printed primary, even when the free actions match it.

### J26: Subhas Chandra Bose [U]

A only below Resistance 20, if a permitted attempt succeeds on at least 3 die faces and success actually raises Resistance; otherwise skip.

Choose the best legal success chance, then most supporting caches, then map order. Use this Event's printed outcome, not the normal displacement outcome; Force 136 and an underground guerrilla are still needed unless the card says otherwise.

### J27: Jungle Conference [U]

A in campaigns 1-2. B early in campaign 3 if an underground guerrilla without Force 136 could use the permission.

Include its extra legal location in ordinary RAIL and STRIKE. It does not grant an additional Special Activity or an extra Raid destination.

### J28: Operation Gustavus [U]

A if a useful Available Force 136 or Available cache can be placed; otherwise D if the Japanese option could remove the last Force 136 or a cache.

Use an eligible Camp Province, no cache already there if a cache will be placed. Safe sites, then a new Force 136 location, then more guerrillas, then map order. Do not create or convert a Camp.

### J29: Kempeitai [U]

A whenever at least 1 Kempeitai remains in the game.

Remove one threatening Force 136 first; then one guarding revealed positive Intel; then one with more Police; then map order. The removal is permanent: do not return it on later turns.

### J30: Malayan Volunteer Corps [U]

A if Police can be removed from at least 2 eligible spaces; B if 1.

Remove exactly one Police from each selected eligible space, within the printed space limit. Use strike priorities for the target order.

### J31: Comfort Women [U]

A if it removes at least 4 Resources; B if it removes 1-3. Skip when no Resources would be lost.

No bot target. Never reduce Resources below zero. Read the physical card for its historical context.

### J32: MPAJU [U]

A if at least 2 useful red placements avoid mixed groups; B if 1. Otherwise skip.

Use only the printed eligible spaces. Do not resolve the Japanese option on behalf of the bot.
